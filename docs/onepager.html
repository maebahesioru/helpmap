HelpMap — find help in your community, in plain language

Purpose

Every community has resources — food banks, free clinics, shelters, ESL classes, legal aid — but the people who need them most often can't find them: scattered PDFs, bureaucratic language, English-only websites. A stressed parent looking for free food tonight doesn't need a search engine; they need a warm, direct answer: where, when, what to bring. HelpMap turns a community's own resource guide into a navigator that answers in plain language — cited to the guide, read aloud, and honest about what it doesn't know.

Key features

  • Guide ingestion — upload the community's resource guide (PDF / TXT / MD); every organization keeps its exact source.
  • Quick help buttons — Food · Health · Shelter · ESL · Legal: one tap answers the most urgent needs.
  • Warm, practical answers — organization, address, hours, languages, and what to bring (including "no ID required"), cited to the exact line.
  • 🔊 Spoken answers — neural TTS reads every answer aloud; accessible for low-vision and low-literacy users.
  • Browse mode — the whole guide by category, with an Ask-about-this button on every organization.
  • Honest refusals — needs the guide doesn't cover get an explicit refusal. An invented address is worse than no answer.
  • Emergency first — danger or medical emergencies are routed to 911 before anything else.
Safety & dignity by design: answers come only from the uploaded guide; no invented addresses or phone numbers, ever. The tone is warm and non-judgmental, and the app surfaces when no ID, no registration, or no documents are required — the questions people are afraid to ask. The bundled guide ("Springfield") is entirely fictional.

How it works

Python + FastAPI backend; single-page web UI (no build step). Guide ingestion keeps source-linked passages; a browse endpoint parses the guide into categorized organizations. Retrieval is scikit-learn TF-IDF (local, any language); generation is retrieval-scoped with mandatory citations and an explicit refusal token. Audio uses edge-tts with content-hash caching. Any OpenAI-compatible LLM endpoint can be configured.

Impact

  • Access — the people who need help most get direct, actionable answers.
  • Language — audio + plain language lower barriers for non-native speakers and low-literacy users.
  • Trust — cited answers and honest refusals make the tool safe to rely on.
  • Reusability — any community can deploy it with their own guide: the data is a file, the app is free.
